# Interactive Webpage Activities for AI Curriculum

This document outlines potential interactive webpages that can be built for each module of the Revised AI Curriculum.

## Module 1: Foundations & Thinking (Weeks 1–5)
* **"Is it AI?" Sorting App (Week 1):** A drag-and-drop quiz webpage where students are presented with different digital tools and must categorize them into "AI-driven" or "Rule-based software". 
* **The Hallucination Hunt Sandbox (Week 2):** A web interface built on top of a basic API where students are tasked with "breaking the chatbot" by deliberately crafting prompts designed to surface errors and document the hallucinations. 
* **AI Pictionary (Week 3):** A prompt-to-image interface where a student receives a secret word, types a prompt to generate an image, and their peers use the generated image to guess the original word. 
* **Filter Bubble Simulator (Week 5):** A simulated, simplified social media feed webpage where the algorithm aggressively adapts to the student's clicks, visually demonstrating algorithmic amplification by quickly trapping them in a filter bubble. 

## Module 2: Creative Exploration (Weeks 6–8)
* **Deepfake Detectives Quiz (Week 6):** An interactive gallery displaying side-by-side real and synthetic media clips. Students can click directly on the images or videos to highlight "tells" and identify the deepfakes.
* **Data Audit Dashboard (Week 7):** A guided interactive survey where students map out where their personal data comes from. The webpage could dynamically generate a visual map or chart of their digital footprint as they answer questions.
* **Mockup Critique Portal (Week 8):** A collaborative digital canvas where groups can upload the visual concepts they generated during their 30-minute mini design sprint, allowing peers to leave digital sticky-note critiques regarding accessibility and dark patterns. 

## Module 3: Applications & Implications (Weeks 9–11)
* **Bias Investigation Sandbox (Week 9):** A webpage simulating a flawed algorithm (like a hiring or loan approval tool). Students input different demographic or background data to test the tool and actively uncover its differential outputs and encoded biases.
* **Career Impact Matrix (Week 10):** An interactive "Will AI take this job?" analysis tool. Students use sliders to rate various professions on traits like "Requires Empathy" or "Repetitive Tasks," and the webpage plots the careers on a scatterplot mapping human necessity versus AI capability.
* **AI Game Mechanic Pitch Deck (Week 11):** A structured web-form that guides students through pitching their AI-powered game concepts. It can prompt them specifically to outline how their procedural generation or NPC behavior adds value, outputting a clean summary card for presentation. 

## Module 4: Project & Reflection (Weeks 12–16)
* **Project Brief & Ethics Pre-Review Tracker (Week 12):** A comprehensive submission portal where students outline their project goal, intended audience, division of labor, and their mandatory ethical pre-review. 
* **Peer Feedback & Milestone Hub (Weeks 13–15):** A centralized dashboard for the three build sessions. Groups can submit their mid-session milestones and access structured peer feedback templates to critique other groups' work-in-progress.
