# Tech With Kev — Homepage Direction Brief

**Prepared by:** Kevin Ng
**Site:** techwithkev.com
**Scope:** Homepage only
**Date:** September 2026

Context and requirements for a homepage redesign, prepared to hand to an external designer for design options.

---

## 01 — The project

Tech With Kev is being repositioned. The current homepage sells one-on-one Math + Python tutoring — that's not really the business anymore. The real work is two built-out courses, **Introduction to AI** and **AI Olympiads Junior**, and the homepage needs to say so.

This brief covers the **homepage only**. The rest of the site (course pages, pricing, contact) still carries the old tutoring branding for now and will be redesigned in a later phase — that's expected, not an oversight.

## 02 — Who Kevin is (the story the page has to tell)

Two true facts, and the page's whole job is making them read as one person, not two unrelated résumés.

- **By day:** an AI & Cloud Solution Architect — currently Lead Value Engineer at SingleStore, previously Senior Solution Architect at Mirantis, with prior roles at HCL and CA Technologies. Over 10 years building and selling real AI, cloud, and LLM systems to enterprises.
- **For 18 years:** a programming and math educator (Meritus Academy, 2007–present) who personally wrote the Introduction to AI and AI Olympiads Junior curricula, and coaches students for Waterloo's Canadian Computing Competition (CCC).

Credentials worth surfacing: Gemini Certified Educator, Certified Kubernetes Administrator (CKA), Claude Academy — Building with the Claude API.

> **Note:** the CCC competitive-programming coaching is a credibility point, not a third product. Don't design it as an equal offer sitting next to the two courses.

## 03 — Who's actually landing on this page

- **Parents** deciding whether an AI/programming course is worth it for their teenager — they need credibility signals fast: real companies, real certifications, a real curriculum, not a stock-photo pitch.
- **Teenagers themselves** — the page needs to feel a little cool, not corporate. Something they'd actually want to poke at.
- **Professional contacts** — the personal-brand angle should hold up as a portfolio piece too, not just a lead funnel.

## 04 — What the page has to do

**Primary goal:** build credibility for the "AI engineer who teaches" story.

**Secondary goal (conversion):** get visitors to register interest in a future cohort of Introduction to AI or AI Olympiads Junior. This is a **waitlist**, not a checkout — there's no live cohort yet; one launches once there's enough registered interest. The CTA copy should stay honest about that rather than implying instant enrollment.

## 05 — Content the design needs to hold

- Wordmark / simple nav (unchanged from the rest of the site for now)
- An early credibility signal — companies worked at, certifications, years teaching
- A hero that states the dual identity plainly
- Two course entries, **equal weight**: Introduction to AI, AI Olympiads Junior
- Three existing interactive visualizers, surfaced as public teasers — real, already-built tools, not mockups. Candidates: an LLM self-attention visualization, a k-means or PCA clustering drag-tool, and a "recommend movies like Netflix" activity. Design should make these feel touchable, not decorative.
- A link to an "AI & logic readiness" interactive quiz (repurposed from an existing diagnostic tool) as a lead-capture piece
- A reserved spot for a YouTube section — the channel exists but has no content yet, so it should look intentional even as a placeholder, with no live video feed required
- One "Register Interest" CTA, repeated more than once down the page
- Room for a parent or student testimonial / quote

## 06 — Directions explored so far (context, not a constraint)

Before bringing in outside design work, three rough directions were sketched internally to sanity-check the positioning. These aren't meant to box anything in — just useful context on what's already been considered.

| Direction | Description |
|---|---|
| **Technical Credibility** | Dark ground, editorial serif headline on a technical background, one warm accent. Leans hard into "real engineer." |
| **Approachable Builder** | Warm, playful, confident — built around the interactive tools as the visual centerpiece. |
| **Editorial Educator** | Magazine/profile treatment — positions this as a story being told, not a product page. |

A live comparison of all three: https://claude.ai/code/artifact/9597adf1-3c54-4abc-995f-d29e58141482

Take it or leave it — the point of sharing it is context, not a brief-within-a-brief.

## 07 — What to explicitly avoid

- The current site's teal/lime "tutoring" look — that's the brand being replaced
- A separate blue-gray "dashboard" visual language used elsewhere on the site for student exercise tools — that belongs to a different product and shouldn't leak into this marketing page
- Generic EdTech/SaaS tropes: purple gradients, a centered hero with stock photography, three-column icon-in-a-circle feature grids

## 08 — Practical constraints

- Static site (Jekyll) — no heavy JS framework required for the homepage itself
- Only existing photo asset today is one portrait of Kevin — assume new photography or assets may follow; don't design around stock photography
- This redesign is homepage-only for now; course, pricing, and contact pages are a follow-up phase

## 09 — What I'm looking for back

A couple of distinct directions to react to — not final, polished comps. Enough to see a real point of view on typography, color, and layout applied to the content above.
