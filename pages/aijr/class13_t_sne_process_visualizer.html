1. Probability Mapping

                

                    t-SNE measures the similarity of points as probabilities. In the high-dimensional space, we place a Gaussian curve over each point. The probability $p_{j|i}$ represents the similarity of point $j$ to point $i$.
                    


                    Perplexity represents the effective number of neighbors. The algorithm adjusts the standard deviation ($\sigma_i$) of the Gaussian around each point individually to match this perplexity, scaling automatically to different local densities.
                

            


            
            

                
02

                
2. The Student-t tail

                

                    When mapping points from high dimensions to 2D, volume scales exponentially. Distant points end up crowded together because the area of a 2D circle is much smaller than the volume of a high-dimensional hypersphere.
                    


                    t-SNE solves this "Crowding Problem" by using a Student-t distribution in the 2D map space. Because its tails are much heavier than a Gaussian, points must go much further apart in 2D to map to the same similarity, allowing distant clusters to segregate cleanly.
                

            


            
            

                
03

                
3. Force Fields & KL

                

                    t-SNE optimizes the coordinates by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between high-D and low-D probabilities. Geometrically, this translates to attraction and repulsion forces between points:
                    


                    • Attraction (Green): Pulls points together if they are similar in high-D but separated in low-D ($p_{ij} > q_{ij}$).
                    


                    • Repulsion (Red): Pushes points away if they are dissimilar in high-D but close in low-D ($p_{ij} < q_{ij}$).
