Underfitting (Bias)

                

                    At Degree 1, the model is a straight line. It fails to capture the underlying wave curve.
                

                

                    This is called **High Bias**. The model makes oversimplified assumptions about the relationship, resulting in high training error and poor predictive performance overall.
                

            


            
            

                
02

                
Overfitting (Variance)

                

                    At Degree 6, the model wiggles wildly to touch every single training point. Its training loss drops to exactly $0.0$.
                

                

                    This is **High Variance**. The model has memorized the noise of the training data. 
                    However, it completely misses the unseen test coordinates because it has failed to generalize.
                

            


            
            

                
03

                
How AI Uses This

                

                    Overfitting is the primary challenge in artificial intelligence development. 
                    Deep neural networks have millions of parameters (analogous to extremely high-degree polynomials).
                

                

                    AI developers prevent overfitting using techniques like **Regularization** (L1/L2 penalty which dampens coefficients) or **Dropout**, forcing models to maintain simple, robust decision logic.
