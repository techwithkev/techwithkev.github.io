What is k-Means?

k-Means is an unsupervised algorithm that partitions data into k groups. It finds clusters by repeatedly assigning each point to the nearest centroid, then moving that centroid to the average of its assigned points.

The Two-Step Loop

① Assign — every data point gets colored by whichever centroid (★) it is closest to, measured by Euclidean distance.

② Move — each centroid jumps to the mean (average x, average y) of all points assigned to it.

Convergence & Inertia

The algorithm stops when centroids no longer move. The quality of a solution is measured by inertia: the sum of squared distances from each point to its centroid. Lower inertia = tighter, more compact clusters.
