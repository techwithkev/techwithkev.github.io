Grayscale & Bit Depth

                

                    In a grayscale image, each pixel is just a single number representing light intensity. 
                    A value of 0 is completely black (no light), and 255 is completely white (full intensity).
                

                

                    Why 255? Because digital images are typically 8-bit. An 8-bit integer can hold exactly $2^8 = 256$ values (0 to 255).
                

            


            
            

                

                    
                

                
Color in Three Dimensions

                

                    To make full color, we layer three separate channels: Red, Green, and Blue (RGB). 
                    Each channel is itself a full 2D array of intensity values.
                

                

                    When stacked together in memory, a color image becomes a 3D Array (Tensor) with a shape of (Height, Width, Channels). 
                    Additive light mixing combines them on your screen.
                

            


            
            

                

                    
                

                
How Computer Vision Sees

                

                    To an AI model (like a CNN or Vision Transformer), an image is not a "picture" — it is just these matrices of numbers. 
                

                

                    A model learns mathematical operations (like 2D convolution filters or attention dot products) that slide over these arrays to find edges, textures, shapes, and eventually classify objects.
